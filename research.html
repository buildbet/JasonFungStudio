Ecommerce Buying-Experience Research

Jason Fung Studio studies how founder-led apparel, hair, beauty and skincare brands can help customers understand, trust and purchase products online.

UpdatedJuly 31, 2026
CategoriesApparel, streetwear, hair, cosmetics and skincare
PurposeTurn category research into better customer journeys

Central finding

The strongest ecommerce experiences reduce the uncertainty blocking the purchase.

Conversion problems are often confidence problems. Customers hesitate when they cannot answer basic questions about fit, shade, ingredients, installation, authenticity, delivery, returns or whether the product is meant for them.

The Growth Sprint uses this research to diagnose the parts of the buying experience customers currently struggle to understand, trust or act on.

Five conclusions

What the research changes about how a store should be built.

01

Confidence beats decoration.

A better store does more than look elevated. It makes the buying decision easier.

02

Each niche has different doubt.

Apparel, hair, cosmetics and skincare shoppers do not evaluate risk the same way.

03

Product pages replace the store visit.

Online pages must compensate for what customers cannot touch, try, test or ask in person.

04

Offers should simplify choice.

The best bundles, sets and routines clarify the right complete purchase, not just the discount.

05

Every change should be testable.

Research creates a hypothesis. Client analytics decide what actually worked.

Method

Observed patterns are useful. They are not magic formulas.

The research reviewed representative leading and high-visibility ecommerce brands, including SKIMS, Gymshark, Alo, Represent, UNice, Luvme Hair, Mayvenn, Indique, Rhode, Rare Beauty, Fenty Beauty, Glossier, The Ordinary and Tower 28.

  • Record what the brand does across navigation, offers, product pages, education, proof, policies and mobile journeys.
  • Identify the customer question or buying objection each pattern appears to solve.
  • Cross-check the pattern against independent ecommerce usability research where possible.
  • Apply the pattern only when the same problem exists in the client's customer journey.
  • Measure the effect using analytics, baseline data and future experiments.

Category findings

Different products create different buying questions.

Apparel

Fit, sizing, quality, fabric, body compatibility, styling and return confidence.

Streetwear

Identity, cultural legitimacy, drop logic, material quality and product context.

Hair and wigs

Installation, lace appearance, density, texture, cap construction, authenticity and care.

Cosmetics

Shade, finish, appearance on different people, application and identity fit.

Skincare

Skin compatibility, ingredients, routine order, safety, efficacy and expected results.

Diagnostic framework

The Buying Experience Score looks at the whole path to purchase.

A store is scored around the customer journey, not a checklist of design trends. The goal is to identify what should change first and what can wait.

20 pts

Offer clarity

What is being sold, who it is for, why it matters and what to buy next.

20 pts

Product confidence

Suitability, fit, quality, use, appearance, delivery and returns.

15 pts

Journey alignment

Consistency from ad to landing page, store, product page, cart, email and SMS.

15 pts

Mobile usability

A clear path for customers arriving through social, email, SMS and paid traffic.

15 pts

Trust and risk

Proof, policies, company information, customer evidence and support access.

10 pts

Merchandising

Logical bundles, sets, complete looks, routines and cross-sells.

5 pts

Measurement readiness

Analytics, campaign tracking, events, baselines and experiment documentation.
